Maple trees grow in temperate regions and typically reach 30–100 feet (9–30 meters) tall. Most species prefer well-drained soil and full sun to partial shade. T heir leaves change to brilliant shades of red, orange, and yellow each autumn, while their winged seeds are carried by the wind to help spread new trees. Maples play an important role in ecosystems by providing food and shelter for birds, insects, and small mammals. They are also widely used by humans—maple wood is popular for furniture and flooring, and sugar maples produce the sap used to make maple syrup. Because of their beauty and shade, maple trees are commonly planted in parks, neighborhoods, and city streets.
